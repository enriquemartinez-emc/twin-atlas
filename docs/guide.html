Using the app

What Twin Atlas is for, what each part of the screen does, and why the list on the side can be long. Written for someone opening the app for the first time.

Start the app

From the top of the repository run ./dev.sh and open http://localhost:4200. The README has the details, and the commands to bring in real map data.

What it is for

You pin information to a real place on a 3D map: a marker for a spot, a note for a spot with a message, an outline for an area. Everyone sees the same place, and you find things by where they are, not by hunting through photos and spreadsheets. Other software can read and add to the same data through the API.

An annotation is the general word for all three. Each has a title, and titles are unique inside a scene (upper or lower case does not matter).

The scene and its green area

A scene is the area you work in. When you make one, it covers whatever the map shows at that moment, and a green rectangle marks it. Everything you add must fall inside that rectangle. If you click outside it, the app tells you where it should go.

The screen, part by part

  1. Scene bar. The app, then the scene you are working in. Pick another from the dropdown, which shows when each scene was made. "New scene" opens a small window: pan the map to the place first, because the new scene covers what you see. On the right, the link button copies the address of what you are looking at, so you can send someone the same scene and annotation, and the cog opens Map settings, where you can paste a free Cesium token to see 3D buildings.
  2. Tools. Select, Marker, Note and Outline decide what a click on the map does. The active tool takes the colour of what it makes. Hold the pointer over one for a line about it. After you save something, the tool goes back to Select.
  3. Hint. A pill above the bottom edge says what to do next with the tool in your hand, for example "Click the map to add corners (2 of 3 so far)". In Select mode it stays away.
  4. Status bar. Beside the tools, at the top, so it never covers the map credits. It says where the map comes from (a dot: green for Google's 3D tiles, amber for the OpenStreetMap globe), where the camera is, and a button that returns to the starting view. While the map loads, a card in the middle of the map says so ("Starting the 3D viewer…", then "Loading the map…") and goes away when the first view is drawn; Google's tiles can take a few seconds.
  5. Panel. It shows one thing at a time. Usually that is the list "In this view", explained next. Choosing a tool swaps the list for a form for something new, and selecting an annotation swaps it for an editor: rename it, change a note's text, or "Move on map" and click the new spot. Cancel or Close brings the list back. Outlines can be renamed but not moved yet. The arrow at the top of the panel hides it and gives the whole screen to the map; a button stays in the corner to bring it back.

Why is the list full of markers?

The list is not a list of everything in the scene. It is everything inside the part of the map you are looking at, so it grows when you zoom out and shrinks when you zoom in. It has several answers to "why so many":

  • You, or someone else, added many. The count above the list ("26 in view") says how many. The filter above the list (All, then a pin, a speech bubble and a hexagon for markers, notes and outlines, each with its count) narrows it, and a click on a row flies to it.
  • They were imported. Real map data from OpenStreetMap (shops, landmarks, building outlines) arrives in bulk, and each place becomes a marker. They carry an "OSM" badge, and their titles end in a reference such as (node/123) or (way/456), or read OSM node/123 when the place has no name. The reference is how the app tells two places called "Bar" apart and notices when you import the same data twice.
  • You are zoomed far out. The list shows at most 200. Past that it says "More annotations are in view than shown. Zoom in to see them all."

On the map, labels appear only when the camera is fairly close, so a crowded view stays readable. The annotation you have selected is always labelled.

A first session

  1. Open the app. With no scenes yet, the top bar says so and the panel asks you to open one.
  2. Pan and zoom to a place, click "New scene" in the top bar, name it, and create it. A green rectangle appears.
  3. Choose Marker in the tools, click inside the green area, give it a title, and Save.
  4. Find it in "In this view", click it to fly to it, and edit it.
  5. Copy the link and open it in another tab: you land on the same scene and annotation.

Two people at once

If someone saves a change to an annotation while you have it open, your save is refused with "Someone changed this annotation since you opened it", and "Load latest version" shows what they did. Nothing is overwritten.

What it does not do yet

  • There is no delete, and no login: whoever opens the app shares one database.
  • There is no import screen. Real map data goes in through the API (see the README).
  • Outlines cannot be moved on the map, only renamed.
